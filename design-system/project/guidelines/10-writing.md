# Writing for PRISM

A reader should finish any page knowing three things: what PRISM does, how far it has come, and why to believe it. Write for an engineer who will check the numbers.

## The message

**Tell us what your part must survive. We deliver the material.** PRISM uses AI to design new alloys and polymers. Then we make them, test them and hand over the proof. Say it in that order: requirement, design, check, make, test, proof.

## Plain words first

Write so that anyone can follow, not only a materials scientist. Short sentences, one idea each. Everyday words: *test sample*, not coupon; *3D printing*, not LPBF; *traceability*, not provenance; *safe settings*, not process window. If a technical term has to appear, explain it in the same sentence. Put the precise terms in source lines and figure notes, where specialists look for them.

The promise behind it, from the deck: *the material should follow the mission, not define its limits.*

## Talk in stacks

Explain the platform as five stacks, each with the problem it solves and the maturity of each layer:

| Stack | Solves | One line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | An effectively infinite design space | Finds the few candidates worth making. |
| Harness | Models that only propose | Runs the science as one system. |
| Autonomy | Experiments as the bottleneck | Takes the human out of the sequence, not out of the loop. |
| Manufacturing and test | Compositions that never become materials | Turns candidates into parts and physical evidence. |
| Evidence | Partners who will not share, results nobody can trace | Keeps every claim provable and every partner in control. |

## Before and after

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| The combinatorial design space is effectively infinite. | Five of nine metals, mixed in steps of 1 %, give 474 million possible alloys. |
| Candidates are melted and printed in industrial processes. | We melt and 3D-print the best ideas. |
| Our revolutionary AI platform | PRISM, a closed-loop materials platform. |
| We qualify new materials. | Evidence assembled for your qualification process. *Target.* |
| Robots run our lab. | Robotic synthesis. *In development.* |
| A named partner under NDA | PFAS-free polymers, with an industrial partner under NDA. *Contracted.* |

## Rules

- Headlines are sentences. Kickers are labels. Leads explain the headline in two or three sentences.
- One claim per row, one number per stat. Each number has a label under it and a source line near it.
- Status words are fixed: Active, Awarded, Contracted, Delivered, Next for programmes; In use, Prototype, In development, Target for capabilities.
- Mark examples as illustrative and use placeholder parties ("Customer A"), never a real partner in an invented scenario.
- Do not name competitors on the website. A precedent is not a competitor: the SX500 foundry story is fair, with its source.
- Do not publish candidate compositions, contract values, export-control classifications or personal contact details.
